# How the parameters of the verification model were reached

This file records every step, in order, so that the manuscript (section 5.4 and the supplementary note) can state how the
values were chosen. All runs are on the 10.56 km cell space (`data/cellspace_bdc_10k.tif`, 77,238 cells,
SHA-256 `a4bd7beb…`), step 1 of the scenario (the first allocation), with `max_iteration = 3000`, unless said otherwise.
Times are for one core of a desktop PC (about 0.16–0.18 s per iteration) and are indicative.

## 1. Purpose and what the model is not

The model exists to exercise the whole workflow (declared open sources → hashed cell space → potential → allocation →
maps) at national scale and to compare the computation with the TerraME/LuccME reference. It is **not** a replica of
LuccME-BR, **not calibrated**, and its maps are **not** land-use projections. Nothing in the acceptance criteria
(`acceptance.toml`) compares it with observed land use: the criteria are demand met, repeatability, and agreement of class
areas across resolutions. The parameters are therefore declared and illustrative, and the paper says so.

## 2. First attempt: the published parameters (S2 of Bezerra et al., 2022)

The potential (rho, constant, coefficients of the drivers that the cell space carries) and the allocation parameters
(static 0, min_change 0.125, saturation thresholds) of S2 were transcribed into the model
(kept in `model/reference/verification_model_s2.toml`). Problems found:

* The S2 sheet of pasture repeats the country-vegetation numbers shifted by one row (same rho 0.950187 and constant
  −0.0138203) and has no `e_urban100`; the values are therefore not usable as published (to ask the authors).
* The drivers S2 uses that this cell space lacks (slope, mcwda, e_connmkt, as_*) are left out, so the potentials are not the
  published ones in any case.
* Result at step 1: with `max_iteration = 1000` the allocation did **not** converge (error 2,170 km²); with 3,000 it was
  accepted at iteration 1,501 with error 1,832 km². The allocation doubles its tolerance after half of `max_iteration`,
  so the run was accepted only under the doubled tolerance (2,000 km²), not under S2's `maxError` of 1,000 km².
* The residual was a transfer between three classes (forest +2,159, mosaic −1,738, country −435 km²), with the sum of gaps
  about 0: the demand is consistent; the dynamics were the problem.

Earlier failures that were data, not model, and are fixed in the pipeline: 61 % NaN in `e_connport` (replaced by the GPM
road/port network); cells whose land uses did not sum to 1 (`complete_cellspace.py`, renormalising partly covered
cells and removing cells below 50 % coverage; a stale file had produced a persistent +48,000 km² gap).

### 2.1 Sweep of the constants of the published model (to test "just a bias")

| Variant | iterations | max error (km²) |
|---|---|---|
| base | 1501 | 1832 |
| mosaic const +0.005 | 1531 | 1950 |
| mosaic const +0.010 | 1596 | 1980 |
| forest const −0.005 | 1501 | 1832 |
| mosaic +0.005 and forest −0.005 | 1531 | 1963 |

No variant converged before the tolerance doubled, and shifting a constant did not help. A single constant is not the
cause. The published parameters were then dropped as the basis of the model.

## 3. The declared parameterisation

Same structure as LuccME-BR (continuous allocation with saturation; potential `const + Σ β·x + rho·lag`), with parameters
generated by `scripts/make_parameters.py` from `model/parameter_weights.toml` (the only hand-chosen numbers) and the
statistics of the 10.56 km cell space (`model/parameters_derivation.json`):

* `rho = 0.90` for every moving class (S2: 0.865–0.95).
* `β_kd = w_kd / σ_d`: `w` is the change of potential for one standard deviation of driver `d`, so every driver
  contributes in the same order of magnitude whatever its unit (metres, shares, counts). `σ_d` is the standard deviation
  of the driver over the mask of the 10.56 km cell space.
* `const_k = (1 − rho)·mean_k − Σ w_kd·μ_d/σ_d`: chosen so that the mean potential of each class is zero at the start.
  This replaces the ad-hoc bisection of an earlier script (`potential_balance.py`) with an analytic rule: with a mean
  potential of zero the allocation has no net push in one direction.
* The statistics are computed once on the 10.56 km cell space and applied unchanged at 5.28 km, so the model is the same
  on both grids.
* `others` does not change (static 1).

### 3.1 Weights (per +1 standard deviation of the driver)

| Class | Driver | Weight | Reason (direction of the usual relation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| forest | e_connport | +0.02 | stays forest far from ports |
| forest | e_uroads | +0.02 | stays forest far from roads |
| forest | c_ucspas | +0.03 | protected areas retain forest |
| country vegetation | e_connport | +0.01 | same direction as forest, weaker |
| country vegetation | e_uroads | +0.01 | idem |
| country vegetation | c_ucspas | +0.02 | idem |
| pasture | e_uroads | −0.02 | expands near roads |
| pasture | e_connport | −0.015 | expands near ports |
| pasture | c_ucspas | −0.03 | not in protected areas |
| agricultural | e_railway | −0.015 | near railways |
| agricultural | e_connport | −0.015 | near ports |
| agricultural | c_ucspas | −0.03 | not in protected areas |
| mosaic | e_urban10 | −0.02 | near urban areas |
| mosaic | c_nusett | +0.02 | near rural settlements (assentamentos rurais) |
| mosaic | e_proads | −0.01 | near roads |
| mosaic | c_ucspas | −0.02 | not in protected areas |
| forestry | e_connport | −0.01 | near ports |
| forestry | c_ucspas | −0.02 | not in protected areas |

Magnitudes (0.01–0.03) are of one order and are **not estimated**: the cell space has a single date, so there is no
observed change to fit. Signs follow the usual direction of these relations in the literature on land-use change in Brazil.
The resulting constants and coefficients are in `model/verification_model.toml` (generated; do not edit by hand).

## 4. Result with the S2 allocation parameters

Declared potentials with S2's allocation (static 0, min_change 0.125): the 2-step run needed 1,501 iterations (max error
883 km² at the end) and the trace showed forest and mosaic oscillating by up to ±11,000 km² around iteration 800, with the
elasticity of mosaic rising to 1.09. The potentials were therefore no longer the problem; the allocation parameters were.

## 5. Allocation parameters

Three variants of the allocation parameters, with the declared potentials (2 steps, step 1 reported):

| Variant | static | min_change | iterations | max error (km²) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S2 | 0 | 0.125 | 1501 | 883 (under the doubled tolerance) |
| A | −1 | 0.125 | 1501 | 1364 |
| B | −1 | 0.05 | 1501 | 1368 |
| **C (adopted)** | **0** | **0.05** | **1048** | **995** |

Only C converged under the tolerance of 1,000 km² without doubling it (the doubling would have started at iteration 1,500).
`min_change` is the smallest per-cell change the allocation considers (S2: 0.125 per step, a per-cell share). With the
declared potentials the dead zone of 0.125 is too wide for the mosaic class, whose potentials are small, and this made the
elasticity climb without effect; 0.05 removes this. It is one of two departures from the S2 allocation parameters (the other is `static`, section 5.1), and
both are stated in the paper as such.

### 5.1 A single allocation is not enough: multi-step test

The tests above reported only the first allocation (`iterations_per_step = [0, N]`: step 0 is the initial state). The
15-step run with variant C failed at the **second** allocation (`step 2`, error 2,676 km²). The trace of that step
showed forest, pasture, agricultural and forestry converging, while **country vegetation (−3,000 km²) and mosaic
(+3,300 km²) stayed locked in opposite directions**; the elasticity of country fell to 0.01.

Cause: the scenario keeps the demand of country vegetation constant (2,086,318 km² every year), yet with `static = 0` the
class may exchange area with the others, and part of it drifts to mosaic. At step 1 this was −1,340 km² (inside the
doubled tolerance), at step 2 −3,000 km².

Fix, a rule and not a number: a class whose demand does not change in the scenario is **fixed** (`static = 1`, as `others`);
the classes that move follow the **direction of their demand** (`static = −1`). Tested on 3 consecutive allocations
(`--steps 4`, `static_by_class` in `model/parameter_weights.toml`, `min_change = 0.05`):

| Variant | static (country / moving classes) | iterations (steps 1, 2, 3) | max error (km²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C (failed at step 2 of the 15-step run) | 0 / 0 | 1048, … | 995, then 2676 |
| D | 1 / 0 | 810, 769, 781 | 989–998 |
| **E (adopted)** | **1 / −1** | **473, 639, 670** | **993–998** |

E converges earlier and keeps the semantics simple: fixed where the scenario is constant, following the demand where it
moves. All three steps converged under the tolerance of 1,000 km² without doubling it.

## 6. Final configuration

* Potentials: section 3 (generated). Allocation: S2's except `min_change = 0.05` and `static` (country fixed = 1, the moving
  classes follow the demand direction = −1; section 5.1).
* Tolerance: `max_difference_km2 = 1000` (S2 `maxError`); `max_iteration = 3000`; accepted if error ≤ 1,000 km²
  (`acceptance.toml`, tightened from 2,000 after the 15-step result below).
* Result, 15 years at 10.56 km (run `own15`, 77,238 cells, desktop PC, one core): **all 14 allocations converged under 1,000 km²**
  (max error per year 842–1,000 km²), iterations per year 473, 639, 670, 697, 737, 746, 802, 877, 912, 928, 956, 1010,
  1051, 1064; **1,694 s (28 min), peak memory 393 MB**. Final-year class errors: forest +997, country −562, mosaic −466,
  pasture +27, agricultural +5, forestry +0.3 km²; `others` 0. `check_run.py`: `demand_met` PASS (determinism and resolution
  checks need the repeat run and the 5.28 km run).
* Reproduce:

```
python scripts/make_parameters.py --cellspace data/cellspace_bdc_10k.tif     # writes model/verification_model.toml
python scripts/run_experiment.py --cellspace data/cellspace_bdc_10k.tif --label try10 --steps 2
```

## 7. What the paper can and cannot claim

* It can claim: the workflow runs end to end on the Brazil grid at two resolutions with declared parameters; the allocation
  meets the demand within a stated tolerance; the run is repeatable; time and memory.
* It cannot claim: that the model reproduces LuccME-BR, that it is calibrated or validated against observed land use, or that
  its maps are projections. The published parameters were tried first, were not usable as published (corrupted pasture
  sheet, drivers absent from the cell space, no convergence under the published tolerance), and are kept as a reference.

## 8. The `ag_apti_*` bands are not used (decision of 9 Oct 2026)

Found while drawing the driver maps (`scripts/make_figures.py`). In the 10.56 km input raster `ag_apti_B` takes only the
values 0 and 1 (mean 0.968) and `ag_apti_MB` only 0 and 2 (mean 1.935); `ag_apti_MB` is exactly twice `ag_apti_B`.
Cause: the aptitude source is a polygon shapefile and, in DisSCube 0.5.0, the class operators (`percentage`, `majority`,
`minority`) applied to a vector source do not filter by attribute: they rasterize every polygon with the value of
`class_code` (`disscube/operators/zonal.py`). The bands therefore mark where the aptitude map has polygons (about 97% of
the cells), multiplied by `class_code`, and do not distinguish fertility classes. (In LuccME-BR, S2 of Bezerra et al., 2022,
the two bands are the percentage of the cell with low and with medium-low agricultural suitability.)

Decision: the three weights that used `ag_apti_MB` (pasture +0.01, agricultural +0.03, forestry +0.015) were removed from
`model/parameter_weights.toml` and the parameters regenerated; the bands stay in the input raster but no potential uses them.
Constants that changed: pasture −0.00145 → +0.05327, agricultural −0.11611 → +0.04805, forestry −0.06067 → +0.02142
(forest, country vegetation and mosaic are unchanged). Check at 10.56 km, first allocations: 485 and 558 iterations, errors
998.5 and 999.9 km² (with the aptitude weights: 473 and 639). The full 15-year result is in section 6 once rerun.
Consequence for the paper: the model has no agricultural-suitability driver, which LuccME-BR does; this is another point
where it is not a replica. Fixing the operator (filtering the polygons by the `FERTILID2` attribute) belongs to DisSCube.
